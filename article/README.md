# Manuscript

This directory contains the manuscript for *Understanding Australian and New Zealand Student Visa Approval Rates Through Origin Composition and Weighting*, the third study in a cumulative research sequence on student-visa decision outcomes.

- [`article.tex`](article.tex) is the manuscript source of record.
- [`article.pdf`](article.pdf) is the compiled reading copy.
- [`bibliography.bib`](bibliography.bib) contains the cited references.

## Research question

The study asks whether Australia's higher average recorded student-visa decision approval rate can be accounted for by origins with higher observed Australian rates contributing larger shares of its decisions. The analysis compares observed destination-specific weights with common origin weights and decomposes the aggregate difference into rate and composition components.

The answer in the analysed 2022–2025 records is no. Applying the same average origin distribution to both destinations widened the Australian-minus-New Zealand difference from 5.3 to 10.9 percentage points. The −5.6-point composition component shows that the observed distribution reduced rather than created the positive average difference.

These are aggregate descriptive accounting results. They do not explain why origin-specific rates differ, establish a causal destination effect, or estimate outcomes for individual applicants.

## Relationship to the preceding study

The preceding matched-origin study established an average but heterogeneous difference in recorded decision approval rates between Australia and New Zealand. It is available as a [Zenodo preprint](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23739706) and in a separate [public repository](https://github.com/vitalii-kaplan/au-nz-comparison).

This manuscript has a distinct contribution centred on origin composition, weighting, formal common-weight standardisation, symmetric decomposition, and annual variation. The shared source data and relationship between the studies must be disclosed, while prose, tables, figures, and principal findings must not be presented as independent discoveries in both articles.

## Build

From the repository root, reproduce the complete analysis and rebuild the PDF with:

```sh
python3 scripts/reproduce_article.py --run-tests --build-pdf
```

To compile only the current manuscript after its inputs are available:

```sh
cd article
latexmk -pdf -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error article.tex
```

The article-only command does not regenerate or validate the analysis results. Before release, run the complete workflow, inspect the LaTeX log for unresolved citations or references, confirm that reported values match the verified outputs, and review the rendered PDF for table and figure legibility.
